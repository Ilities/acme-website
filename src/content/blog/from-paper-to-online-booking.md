---
title: Moving from a paper calendar to online booking
description: How Studio Lumo, a two-chair hair salon in Tampere, switched to online booking without losing a single appointment.
pubDate: 2026-02-03
author: Mikko Laine
---

Riikka and Tuomas have run Studio Lumo in Tampere for eleven years. Until last winter, every appointment lived in a large paper calendar on the counter.

"It worked fine, as long as one of us was there to answer the phone," Riikka says. "The problem was that we were usually busy cutting hair."

## Starting small

They did not switch everything over in one go. For the first two weeks, they kept the paper calendar and only opened online booking for Tuomas's evening appointments. That gave them time to get the service list and appointment lengths right before regular clients started using it.

Our support team imported their customer list from a spreadsheet Riikka had been keeping alongside the paper calendar, so returning clients did not have to re-enter their details.

## What changed

After two months, around 60 percent of their bookings were made online, most of them outside opening hours. Phone calls during the working day dropped to a handful.

"The biggest surprise was how many people book at eleven at night," Tuomas says. "Those were people who would never have called us."

## Advice for others

If you are thinking about making the same move:

- Set up your services and appointment lengths carefully first. It is the part that takes the most thought.
- Run both systems side by side for a week or two.
- Tell regular clients in person, and put the booking link on a small card at the counter.
- Keep a few slots off the online calendar for regulars who prefer to call.

Studio Lumo now runs entirely on Acme Book. The paper calendar is in a drawer, "just in case", Riikka says.
